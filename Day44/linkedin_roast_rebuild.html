Part 4 — 7-Day LinkedIn Activation Plan

Day 1

Make All Profile Changes

  • Add the 10 recommended skills; remove/de-prioritize Prompt Engineering

  • Add a profile photo + banner if you don't have one (professional headshot, plain background)

Day 2

Publish Post #1 — "I Just Rebuilt My LinkedIn"

I just realized my LinkedIn headline said "Student at [my university]" for the past year. That's it. No skills. No projects. Nothing a recruiter could search for. So I rebuilt it — and while doing it, I really looked at the AI project I'd been sitting on: Bhumiputra, a tool that classifies soil type from a photo and recommends crops + fertilizer, with voice guidance in English and Hindi for farmers who don't read English comfortably. I'd basically buried it under generic "eager to learn" language. Lesson: your profile isn't a resume PDF. It's a search result. If it doesn't say what you can DO, in words people actually search, it doesn't matter how good the work is. Currently open to ML/AI and software dev internships — link to the project in comments 👇

Under 1300 characters. The hook ("headline said Student at [university] for a year") drives comments because it's specific and slightly self-deprecating — people relate and reply with their own headline mistakes.

Day 3

Send 10 Strategic Connection Requests

WHO to target: (1) ML/AI recruiters at companies hiring interns in your city/region, (2) engineers currently working as ML/Data interns or junior engineers — they refer, (3) alumni from your university now in tech roles, (4) people who post about AgriTech or accessible AI (your niche overlap).

Hi [Name], I'm a CS student building AI projects in the agri-tech space (soil classification + crop recommendation with TensorFlow). Really admire your work at [Company] — would love to connect and follow your journey in ML.

Under 300 characters. Personalize [Name]/[Company] every time — generic requests get ignored or reported.

Day 4

Comment on 5 Posts in Your Target Space

The Value Comment Formula: Agree + Add Insight + Ask a Question.

Example: "Agree that deployment is where most ML projects die. I hit this with a CNN soil classifier — Streamlit got it usable fast, but I'm curious how you handle model versioning once you have real users. Do you retrain on a fixed schedule or trigger on drift?"

Why it works: it proves competence (you've shipped something), invites a reply (the question), and doesn't just flatter the poster.

Day 5

Publish Post #2 — Lessons-Learned / Hot Take

Unpopular opinion: most student AI projects fail not because the model is bad, but because nobody thought about who'd actually use it. When I built a soil classification tool for farmers, the model was the easy part. The real work was: — Adding voice guidance in Hindi, because reading English isn't a given — Keeping the UI to one screen, because nobody wants to "learn" a farming tool — Testing on a phone, not a laptop, because that's what's actually in someone's hand in a field We (CS students) love optimizing accuracy by 2%. We rarely ask who's on the other end of the screen. What's a project where you learned this the hard way?

Hot-take framing + direct question at the end = comment magnet.

Day 6

Engage + Send 5 More Requests

  • Reply to every comment on Day 2 and Day 5 posts within a few hours — early engagement boosts reach
  • Send 5 more connection requests using the same template, targeting a new set of companies/recruiters
Day 7

Review & Adjust

What to check: Profile views (up = your headline/photo is getting clicked in search), Search appearances (up = your keywords are working), Connection acceptance rate (below ~30% = your request message needs work, not your profile).

What to adjust: If profile views are flat, revisit your headline keywords. If people accept but don't reply to messages, your opening line after connecting needs more specificity (reference their actual work, not a template).
