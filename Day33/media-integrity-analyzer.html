Welcome

Learn to spot the tricks media uses on you

Every day, headlines and posts compete for your attention. Some inform you honestly. Others exaggerate, distort, or push your emotional buttons to get a click, a share, or a reaction. This isn't about being cynical — it's about being aware, so you can decide what to trust and what to question.

You'll work through two short challenges. In each one, you'll look at a real-feeling example, make a judgment call, and then see the full breakdown — including what was actually going on beneath the surface.
Challenge 1 · Headline Detective

Can you spot a misleading headline?

Headlines are written to earn a click — sometimes at the expense of accuracy. Words like "shocking," "destroys," "secret," or vague claims ("scientists say...") can stretch the truth. Recognizing this helps you decide whether a headline reflects the real story, or oversells it.

Why it matters: misleading headlines shape public opinion before people even read the article — and many people never read past the headline at all.

Challenge 2 · Emotion Detector

What is this post making you feel — and why?

Content creators and advertisers know that emotion drives engagement more than facts do. Fear, outrage, envy, and urgency all make you stop scrolling and react. Learning to notice how a post makes you feel — and which specific words caused it — is one of the strongest media literacy skills you can build.

Why it matters: once you can name the emotional lever being pulled, it loses much of its power over your judgment.

Three practical media literacy habits

  • 1Read past the headline before reacting or sharing — check whether the article actually supports the claim being made.
  • 2Notice your emotional reaction first. If you feel a strong pull of fear, anger, or excitement, pause before you engage.
  • 3Check the source and look for a second, independent source before treating a claim as fact.
